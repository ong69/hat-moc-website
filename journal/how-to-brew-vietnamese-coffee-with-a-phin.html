How to brew Vietnamese coffee with a phin

A simple step-by-step method for a rich cup without an espresso machine.

  • Step 2: Bloom the grounds
  • Step 3: Let it drip

You do not need an espresso machine to make a rich Vietnamese coffee. You need a clean phin, suitable ground coffee, hot water and a cup. Start with around 20 g of coffee and 80–100 mL of water, then adjust to your filter and taste. The exact volume depends on the size of your phin. If you are making iced coffee, have ice and condensed milk ready before the drip begins.

Step 1: Prepare the filter

Check that the perforations are clear. Rinse the phin with hot water to warm the metal, then discard that water. Put the phin on a sturdy, heatproof cup. Add the ground coffee and gently level it. Avoid tamping hard as though you were making espresso; too much compression can prevent the water from flowing.

Step 2: Bloom the grounds

Pour a small amount of hot water over the grounds, enough to wet them evenly. Wait around 30 seconds. Water just off the boil is a useful starting point, though a slightly cooler temperature may taste better with some darker roasts. Fit the press according to your phin's design, taking care not to overtighten it.

Step 3: Let it drip

Add the remaining water and place the lid on top. The coffee should emerge gradually. Some phins finish in a few minutes; others take longer. Rather than chasing an exact number, look for a controlled drip and taste the result. If the chamber empties almost immediately, the grind may be too coarse or the coffee bed too loose. If nothing moves, it may be too fine or compressed.

Step 4: Make it your way

For cà phê sữa đá, stir the concentrated coffee into condensed milk and pour over plenty of ice. Start with one or two teaspoons of condensed milk and increase to taste; there is no need to make it sweeter than you enjoy. For black coffee, sip a little before adding water or ice. If you want a softer drink, dilute it gradually rather than losing the coffee flavour all at once.

Troubleshooting: too bitter, too weak or too slow

If the coffee is bitter and harsh, try a coarser grind, less coffee or a slightly cooler brew. If it tastes watery, try a finer grind or a higher dose. If the filter stalls, clean the holes and check that the press is not too tight. Change one thing per brew. Otherwise you will not know which adjustment helped.

Three mistakes worth avoiding

First, do not assume every phin needs the same grind. Second, do not judge the drink before stirring: concentrated coffee and condensed milk can sit in distinct layers. Third, clean and dry the metal filter thoroughly after use so old coffee oils do not spoil tomorrow's cup.

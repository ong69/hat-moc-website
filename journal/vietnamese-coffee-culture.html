Vietnamese coffee is more than a drink

From the slow drip of a phin to the layers of a Hanoi egg coffee, Vietnamese coffee makes time for the cup.

In this guide

  • A brewer that sits on the glass
  • Strong coffee meets milk and ice
  • There’s room for different tastes
  • Try the ritual at home

A brewer that sits on the glass

The phin is a small metal filter with a perforated chamber and a press. It rests directly over a cup as hot water works through the grounds. You can hear the first drops if the room is quiet. That slow pace is part of the pleasure, whether you’re at a café or brewing at the kitchen bench.

Strong coffee meets milk and ice

Cà phê sữa đá brings together concentrated coffee, sweetened condensed milk and ice. Coffee and milk can arrive layered in the glass, with the ice separate or already added. Stirring changes each sip, and the drink lightens as the ice melts. A short brew helps the coffee hold its place.

There’s room for different tastes

In Hanoi, cà phê trứng pairs coffee with a whipped egg topping. In Huế, cà phê muối pairs a strong brew with salted cream. Bạc xỉu takes the cup in a different direction, giving the milk most of the room and using coffee for flavour. Each one is a distinct way to balance the brew.

Try the ritual at home

Put the condensed milk in a heatproof glass, set the phin over it and watch a full brew before changing your recipe. Note the grind and how quickly the coffee drips. Taste it before adding ice. Those few minutes give you a better feel for what to change next time.

Why Vietnamese Robusta coffee tastes different

Understand the body, bitterness and roast behind a strong Vietnamese coffee, then adjust your brew to suit your taste.

In this guide

  • Robusta and Arabica in the cup
  • Why it works with condensed milk
  • How to adjust a phin brew
  • What our label tells you

Robusta and Arabica in the cup

Robusta often gives coffee a fuller body and a more pronounced bitterness. Arabica can bring brighter acidity and a wider range of fruit or floral notes. Roast and preparation have a big say in both, so origin and species are only part of the picture.

Why it works with condensed milk

The thicker texture and strong roast flavours can still come through after you add sweetened condensed milk. If the coffee disappears after a few minutes over ice, try a shorter extraction volume on your next cup or use larger ice cubes to slow dilution.

How to adjust a phin brew

Start with about 18 g of coffee and 90 mL of hot water. Wet the grounds first with part of the water, then pour the rest. If water barely drips, grind slightly coarser or ease off the press. If the cup tastes weak and the water ran straight through, try a finer grind.

What our label tells you

Hạt Mộc’s Hanoi blend lists Robusta, Culi (peaberry) and Moka. The manufacturer references Đắk Lắk and Lâm Đồng. Start with a short black taste and then add a little milk if you like. That side-by-side taste tells you more about how the blend suits you than judging it only in a sweet drink.

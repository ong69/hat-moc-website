Cashew guides

Five easy ways to enjoy roasted cashews

From afternoon snacks to salads, here are simple ways to enjoy their crunch.

THE PRACTICAL GUIDE

Five ways to make a handful go further

Fresh and bright

Add chopped cashews to a lime-dressed herb and cabbage salad.

Warm and savoury

Scatter over stir-fried vegetables or a rice bowl just before serving.

A little indulgence

Pair with a strong iced Vietnamese coffee for a satisfying afternoon break.

Worth knowing

For the best texture, add roasted cashews at the end of cooking. If they are already salted, reduce other salty ingredients and taste before serving.

Roasted cashews are rich, buttery and naturally satisfying, but they can also do a lot of work in everyday cooking. Their crunch contrasts beautifully with fresh herbs, tangy dressings and soft noodles. You do not need a complicated recipe. A handful added at the right moment can turn something ordinary into a dish you actually look forward to eating.

1. Make a Vietnamese-inspired crunchy salad

Toss shredded cabbage, carrot, cucumber, mint and coriander with lime juice, a little sugar and fish sauce or a suitable vegetarian alternative. Finish with roughly chopped roasted cashews just before serving. The dressing brings brightness while the cashews add texture. Add cooked chicken or tofu if you want to make it a meal. Keep the nuts separate until the last minute to prevent them softening.

2. Finish a quick garlic stir-fry

Cook your favourite vegetables over high heat with garlic, then add a splash of soy sauce and a little water. Turn off the heat before folding through roasted cashews. This keeps their flavour noticeable and stops them becoming soggy. Try broccoli, capsicum and snow peas for a simple combination. If the cashews are already salted, taste before adding more seasoning.

3. Build a better afternoon snack

Pair a small handful of cashews with fresh fruit such as mandarin segments or apple slices. The contrast of sweet, juicy fruit and crunchy nuts is more interesting than eating either on its own. For an easy packed snack, keep the nuts in a separate sealed container so they do not absorb moisture from the fruit.

4. Add texture to noodles or rice

Use chopped cashews as a finishing garnish for rice bowls, vermicelli salads or lightly dressed noodles. Fresh herbs, chilli and lime work particularly well. Sprinkle them over the finished dish rather than cooking them for a long time in sauce. You get a satisfying crunch in every few bites without needing to use a large quantity.

5. Pair them with a Vietnamese coffee break

A strong phin coffee and a few roasted cashews make a simple afternoon combination. The coffee's bold flavour plays against the nuts' roasted sweetness. If you drink cà phê sữa đá, try a lightly salted cashew alongside it. Serve the nuts in a small bowl and keep the remaining pack sealed to preserve freshness.

One useful buying tip

Compare ingredients as well as price. Plain roasted cashews, salted cashews and flavoured versions behave differently in recipes. If you are cooking with them, a simpler seasoning often gives you more flexibility. Cashews are also a tree-nut allergen, so always check suitability when sharing food.

An easy two-minute cashew topping

Roughly chop a small handful of roasted cashews. Mix with chopped mint or coriander, a little lime zest and a pinch of chilli if you like heat. Sprinkle over noodles, grilled chicken, tofu or a crunchy salad immediately before eating.
